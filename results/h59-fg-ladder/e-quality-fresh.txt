PCIe Gen3 x16 is a high-speed serial computer expansion bus standard that utilizes sixteen parallel lanes to transmit data between a host controller and a peripheral device. Each individual lane operates at a signaling rate of 8 gigatransfers per second (GT/s), which is achieved through the use of 128b/130b encoding to improve bandwidth efficiency compared to previous generations. Consequently, the raw aggregate data rate for the sixteen lanes is 128 gigatransfers per second, translating to a theoretical maximum unidirectional throughput of approximately 15.75 gigabytes per second (GB/s) after accounting for encoding overhead and protocol framing.

The physical implementation of this interface typically employs a differential signaling scheme, where each lane consists of two wires carrying complementary signals to reduce electromagnetic interference and improve signal integrity over longer distances. To maintain synchronization and data integrity, the protocol incorporates a link training and status state machine (LTSSM) that negotiates the operating speed and width during initialization. Furthermore, PCIe Gen3 supports advanced power management features, allowing the link to enter low-power states when idle, thereby reducing overall system energy consumption without sacrificing performance during active data transfer. This combination of high bandwidth, low latency, and robust error correction makes PCIe Gen3 x16 the standard interface for high-performance components such as graphics processing units (GPUs), solid-state drives (SSDs), and network interface cards (NICs).
