PCIe Gen3 x16 is a high-speed serial computer expansion bus standard that utilizes sixteen parallel lanes to transmit data between a host controller and a peripheral device. Each individual lane operates at a signaling rate of 8 gigatransfers per second (GT/s), which is achieved through the use of 128b/130b encoding to improve bandwidth efficiency compared to previous generations. Consequently, the raw aggregate data rate for the sixteen lanes is 128 gigatransfers per second, translating to a theoretical maximum unidirectional throughput of approximately 15.75 gigabytes per second (GB/s) after accounting for encoding overhead and protocol framing.

The physical implementation of PCIe Gen3 x16 typically involves a differential signaling scheme where each lane consists of two wires: one for transmitting data and one for receiving data, allowing for full-duplex communication. To maintain signal integrity at these high frequencies, the standard employs advanced equalization techniques, including transmitter pre-emphasis and receiver de-emphasis, to mitigate signal degradation caused by insertion loss in the printed circuit board traces. Furthermore, the link initialization process includes a training phase where the transmitter and receiver negotiate the optimal equalization settings to ensure reliable data transmission across the entire channel.

In practical applications, PCIe Gen3 x16 is predominantly used for high-bandwidth peripherals such as graphics processing units (GPUs), solid-state drives (SSDs), and network interface cards (NICs). The architecture supports hot-plugging, power management, and error reporting mechanisms, making it a robust and versatile interface for modern computing systems. Despite the high data rates, the standard maintains backward compatibility with earlier PCIe generations, allowing Gen3 devices to operate at Gen2 or Gen1 speeds if connected to older host controllers, although this results in reduced bandwidth.
